We asked whether a learned page-replacement policy is implementable inside an operating
system kernel. In xv6, it is: \textbf{the kernel already sees enough}. A small linear
score over accessed-bit history and refault bookkeeping, computed in whole numbers,
incurs 8.6\% fewer page faults than \clock at 10\% memory across 14 workloads and is level
with ARC, the strongest classical policy we could run in the kernel; heavier models are
not clearly better and cost 20--150$\times$ as much. What remains is decision time,
55$\times$ \clock's per eviction, which sampling halves. \todo{claim pending D2;
