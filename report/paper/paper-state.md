## Positioning (Step 1)

- **Why heuristics fail:**
  - Belady needs 43–54% fewer faults than Clock on btree/kv/graph, 53–59% on LZW (simulated).
  - In kernel form, the best classical policy (ARC) gains only about 5% over Clock.
- **Why ML, which kind:** imitation of Belady with offline labels. With kernel signals only, capacity does
  not pay. Oracle inputs help on familiar workloads and hurt on real traces.
- **Closest work and delta:** learned caching (Hawkeye, Glider, Parrot, LRB, LeCaR, CACHEUS) sees every
  access, uses floating point and is judged per request. This paper: in-kernel, whole numbers, against kernel
  forms of ARC/LHD/S3-FIFO. **VERIFY:** in-kernel learned eviction work at OS venues, e.g. eBPF page-cache
  policies; the collaborator's notes `cache_ext_notes.md` are not in git.

  1. *Teaching OS.* No Linux experiment. Answer with the feature map, the Linux-recorded Redis/SQLite traces
     (simulator) and plain limits.
  2. *Fault cost assumed.* Instruction counts are exact; the break-even rests on 8 ms / 100 µs and 1 ns per
     instruction. A real-hardware page-in measurement would remove the assumption.
  3. *"Level with ARC" is a modest headline.* The paper's strength is honesty and breadth: 14 workloads,
     intervals, exact costs, and heavier models in the kernel.

  5. *Adapted classical policies.* They are necessary: a kernel cannot see hits. Say so, and do not quote the
